Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we will learn how to calculate the discount margin for floating-rate bonds using QuantLib Python. The discount margin is the spread over the reference rate (like Euribor) that makes the bond's theoretical price equal to its market price.

This is a classic inverse problem: we know the market price and want to find the spread that produces it. We'll use numerical solvers (Brent's method) to find the discount margin and convert between different compounding conventions.

What You'll Learn:
  • Understanding discount margins for floating-rate bonds
  • Building floating-rate bonds with Euribor as reference rate
  • Using numerical solvers for inverse calculations
  • Converting between different interest rate conventions

Key Concepts

Floating-Rate Bonds

Bonds with coupon rates that reset periodically based on a reference rate like Euribor. The coupon payments adjust to reflect current market conditions.

Discount Margin

The spread over the reference rate that reflects the bond's credit risk and liquidity. It's the additional yield required by investors above the risk-free rate.

Inverse Problems

Mathematical problems where we know the result (market price) and need to find the input (discount margin) that produces it. We use numerical solvers to find the solution.

Numerical Solvers

Algorithms like Brent's method that find roots of equations numerically. They're essential for solving complex financial problems where analytical solutions don't exist.

Technical Implementation

The implementation involves several key steps:

  1. Bond Construction: Create a floating-rate bond with Euribor 6M as the reference rate
  2. Curve Setup: Establish forecast and discount curves with appropriate spreads
  3. Pricing Engine: Use DiscountingBondEngine to calculate bond prices
  4. Numerical Solver: Apply Brent's method to find the discount margin
  5. Rate Conversion: Convert between continuous and simple compounding conventions
