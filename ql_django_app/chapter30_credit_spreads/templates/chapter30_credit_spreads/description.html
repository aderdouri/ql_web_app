Chapter Overview

In this chapter, we will explore how to value bonds with credit spreads using QuantLib. We'll demonstrate different approaches to incorporate credit risk into bond pricing, including parallel and non-parallel shifts of yield curves.

We'll start with a simple example using a flat treasury yield curve and then show how to add credit spreads using three different methods: direct rate shocks, ZeroSpreadedTermStructure for parallel shifts, and SpreadedLinearZeroInterpolatedTermStructure for non-parallel shifts.

What You'll Learn:
  • Understanding credit spreads and their impact on bond pricing
  • Implementing parallel shifts using ZeroSpreadedTermStructure
  • Creating non-parallel shifts with SpreadedLinearZeroInterpolatedTermStructure
  • Comparing different credit spread methodologies

Key Concepts

Credit Spread

The additional yield required above the risk-free treasury rate to compensate investors for credit risk. Measured in basis points (bps).

Parallel Shift

A uniform shift of the entire yield curve by the same amount across all maturities. Uses ZeroSpreadedTermStructure.

Non-Parallel Shift

A varying shift where different maturities experience different spread changes. Uses SpreadedLinearZeroInterpolatedTermStructure.

Practical Applications

Direct Rate Shocks

Simple approach where the base rate is directly modified. Useful for flat yield curves and quick sensitivity analysis.

Structured Approaches

Using QuantLib's specialized term structure classes for more sophisticated credit spread modeling and risk management.
