Understanding the Results
Why Different Prices?

The Heston and BSM models give different prices because they make different assumptions about volatility:

  • BSM: Assumes constant volatility throughout the option's life
  • Heston: Models volatility as a stochastic process that changes over time
What This Means

The difference of {{ results.difference_formatted }} shows the impact of stochastic volatility modeling:

  • Positive difference: BSM prices higher (constant vol assumption)
  • Negative difference: Heston prices higher (stochastic effects)
  • Zero difference: Both models agree (rare case)
Practical Implications

For Risk Management: The Heston model provides more realistic pricing for options, especially in volatile markets. The difference you see represents the "volatility smile" effect - how market-observed option prices differ from constant volatility assumptions.
