Understanding Irregular Bonds

Irregular bonds are financial instruments that don't follow standard payment schedules. They can have gaps in coupon payments, mixed fixed and floating rates, or irregular first coupons. This lab demonstrates three common types of irregular bond structures used in real-world financial markets.

Case 1: Last Coupon Before Maturity

This bond has a final coupon payment that occurs one period before the actual maturity date.

What is a Last Coupon Gap Bond?

This type of bond has an irregular payment schedule where the final coupon payment occurs one period before the actual maturity date. This creates a gap between the last coupon payment and the bond's maturity, which affects the bond's pricing and cashflow structure.

  • Fixed Rate: Pays a constant coupon rate throughout the bond's life
  • Floating Rate: Coupon rate adjusts based on market interest rates (e.g., Euribor)

Case 2: Fixed-to-Floating Rate Bond

This bond pays a fixed coupon rate for the first N years, then switches to a floating rate for the remaining period.

What is a Fixed-to-Floating Rate Bond?

This hybrid bond structure combines the stability of fixed-rate payments with the flexibility of floating-rate payments. It's commonly used in corporate finance and structured products where issuers want to manage interest rate risk over different time horizons.

  • Fixed Period: Pays a predetermined coupon rate for the initial years
  • Floating Period: Coupon rate adjusts based on market rates plus a spread

Case 3: Stub Coupon Bond

This bond has an irregular first coupon period that doesn't follow the standard annual schedule.

What is a Stub Coupon Bond?

A stub coupon bond has an irregular first coupon period that doesn't align with the bond's regular payment schedule. This often occurs when bonds are issued between regular coupon dates or when payment schedules are adjusted for market conventions.

  • Irregular First Period: The first coupon covers a non-standard time period
  • Floating Rate Structure: Typically uses floating rates to accommodate irregular periods
