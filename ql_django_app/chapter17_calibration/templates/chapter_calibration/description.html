What Do These Parameters Mean?

Basic Parameters:
a (alpha) - Mean Reversion Speed

Controls how quickly interest rates return to their long-term average.

  • • a = 0.1 : Slow, gentle reversion
  • • a = 0.5 : Fast, strong reversion
σ (sigma) - Volatility Level

Controls the randomness and unpredictability of rate movements.

  • • σ = 0.01 : Low volatility, stable rates
  • • σ = 0.05 : High volatility, erratic rates
Advanced Parameters (G2++):
b, η (eta) - Second Factor

Additional factors that influence interest rate dynamics.

  • • b : Mean reversion speed of second factor
  • • η : Volatility of second factor
ρ (rho) - Correlation

How the two factors interact with each other.

  • • ρ = 0 : Independent factors
  • • ρ = 0.5 : Factors move together
  • • ρ = -0.5 : Factors move in opposite directions
Simple Analogy

Think of driving a car: a is like the steering wheel force that pulls you back to the center of the road, σ is like bumps on the road that make you deviate, and ρ is like how wind and bumps interact to influence your path.

Key Concepts

Hull-White 1-Factor

Hull-White model was one of the first practical exogenous models that attempted to fit to the market interest rate term structures.

drt = (θ(t) - art)dt + σdWt
  • a: mean reversion constant
  • σ: volatility parameter
  • θ(t): fitted to term structure
Black-Karasinski

Black-Karasinski is not an affine model, and hence we cannot use the JamshidianSwaptionEngine. We use the TreeSwaptionEngine which works with all short rate models.

dln(rt) = (θt - a ln(rt))dt + σdWt
  • Log-normal model
  • Ensures positive rates
  • Tree-based pricing
G2++ 2-Factor

G2++ is a two-factor model that allows for more complex interest rate dynamics. It includes correlation between the two factors, providing better fit to market data.

dxt = -axtdt + σdWt1
dyt = -bytdt + ηdWt2
  • Two correlated factors
  • Better volatility fitting
  • More complex calibration

Calibration Methods

Standard Calibration

Optimize all model parameters to fit market data.

  • • Full parameter optimization
  • • Best fit to market
  • • Most flexible approach
Constrained Calibration

Fix certain parameters while optimizing others.

  • • Fixed reversion rate
  • • Reduced complexity
  • • Faster computation
Normal Volatility

Use normal volatilities for negative rate environments.

  • • European/Japanese markets
  • • Negative rate handling
  • • Market consistency
